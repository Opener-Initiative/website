***One day. Six stacks. A community exploring DECT NR+ together.***

On November 2, ahead of DECT World 2026, participants from Europe and beyond gathered in Munich for "Getting Started with NR+". A packed room set the tone for a day of technical exchange and hands-on exploration.

## Making NR+ Accessible

The OPENER Initiative organized the pre-event together with the [DECT Forum](https://www.dect.org). We designed the event to be accessible and informal, giving newcomers and experienced developers space to exchange ideas and expertise, and to explore questions that rarely fit into a conference programme. The event was open to anyone interested in DECT NR+, from first-time learners to those already building with the technology.

## From Talks to Toolchains

A series of introductory talks set the stage for the hands-on sessions. Roel Ottink (DECT Forum) and Prof. Dr Mª Dolores Pérez Guirao (OPENER Initiative, [Ostfalia HAW](/members/ostfalia/)) welcomed participants. Dr Andreas Wilzeck (Sennheiser) provided an overview of DECT NR+, and stack and hardware providers introduced the ecosystem and the technologies available to explore.

After lunch, the Experience Area opened for the rest of the afternoon, with a short coffee break in between. Teams at six islands offered guided demos and expert Q&A. Participants could work on their own laptops and log in to Raspberry Pis with a preinstalled toolchain. We ended the day with informal networking over pizza and beer.

## Six Islands, Six Stacks

Across six islands, participants could learn about different stacks and explore the NR+ ecosystem first-hand.

STRATUM 9 showed their NR+ stack with a focus on a deterministic MAC layer for industrial use. DSR presented their OneNR+ software stack, including a demo of Matter over DECT NR+. QARTIA showcased a complete stack, from custom miniature hardware and a high-performance MAC layer to IPv6 routing, real-time video streaming, and mesh networking.

Manulytica presented their DECT NR+ firmware library for Zephyr RTOS, designed for low-latency networking in industrial, robotics, and machinery settings. Participants tested the library using an interactive management console and shell.

hophop (Silano Systems) demonstrated example applications running DECT NR+ firmware on Ariel OS and connecting embedded devices and PCs over IP via DECT NR+. Participants could exchange network traffic between PCs, access the devices' applications and DECT configuration, extend those applications, and monitor traffic in Wireshark using hophop's sniffer application.

Representing the OPENER Initiative, [Codium](/members/codium/) ran the Opener stack on their DECT NR+ hardware, and [Deveritec](/members/deveritec/) brought their dectnrPi Raspberry Pi HAT and a DECT NR+ Wireshark sniffer. Participants could bring their own code and debug it on the hardware.

The islands filled up right after the talks, and the teams had their hands full answering questions. Participants moved from demo to demo, and everything ran smoothly.

## The People Behind NR+

Participants asked questions, shared ideas, and exchanged expertise throughout the day. They expressed appreciation for the opportunity and the organization of the event. As Wienke Giezeman, CEO and Co-Founder of The Things Industries, put it: "Cool people doing cool things."

Feedback highlighted the value of meeting the people behind NR+, from the wider community to the companies developing the technology. Respondents described gaining a clearer understanding of where NR+ stands and how industry uses it. They also highlighted hands-on experience with real hardware and the breadth of the NR+ developer ecosystem.

The speakers and the teams running the demos valued the preparation and collaboration and expressed willingness to support another event. Karoline Kopka of STRATUM 9 called the day "an excellent kick-off to a potential event series".

## What the Community Wants Next

Among the responses, technical deep dives and hands-on workshops were the most requested formats, followed by use-case sessions and an online follow-up. Contributors to the pre-event also expressed interest in a joint interoperability test session.

This feedback provides a starting point for future activities.
