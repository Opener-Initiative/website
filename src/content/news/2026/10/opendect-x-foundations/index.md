OpenDECT-X is easy to misread at a glance: a project with "DECT" in the name, three partners of very different kinds, and public funding attached to it. Put together, these three facts explain what the project is and why it exists.

## Who is building it

OpenDECT-X brings together three partners with complementary capabilities, working towards one goal: an open ecosystem for DECT NR+ in Europe.

**Ostfalia University of Applied Sciences** coordinates the project and acts as the neutral intermediary between the consortium and external stakeholders. **Sennheiser** defines the DECT NR+ profile for audio applications, contributing the security and latency requirements it has built up over decades of experience in wireless professional audio. **Last Mile Semiconductor** brings chip and hardware expertise and is responsible for the open stack architecture.

**Nordic Semiconductor** joins as an associated partner: the first vendor to bring DECT NR+ into a commercially available System-in-Package (the nRF9161, in 2024), contributing SDKs and hands-on embedded experience.

This combination is no coincidence: an open ecosystem needs research, applications, and silicon from day one.

## What it actually is

DECT is widely associated with the cordless phone. Its standardization began at CEPT and ETSI in 1988; the first ETSI standard, EN 300 175, was published in 1992. DECT NR+ carries a related name. It shares little else.

> DECT NR+ is a clean-sheet radio redesign that uses modern digital transmission techniques while coexisting with legacy DECT in the same frequency band.
>
> --- Dr. Andreas Wilzeck, Sennheiser

In practice: a completely new radio design built on current transmission techniques, able to operate alongside existing DECT deployments. Designed not for telephony, but for massive IoT, industrial and building automation, and professional audio. Since October 2021, DECT NR+ has been officially recognized by the ITU-R as an IMT-2020 (5G) technology.

Understanding this distinction matters. Those who dismiss DECT NR+ as "the cordless phone standard" are missing one of the most relevant wireless technologies for European industrial sovereignty. With OpenDECT-X, we are creating an open foundation that anyone can build on.

## Why it is publicly funded

Why does an open standard need public funding when the companies involved are financially strong? Because open-source infrastructure for industrial wireless does not emerge from a market incentive. No single company has a sufficient interest in building a reference implementation that is freely available to all, including its own competitors.

Mareike Zocher, Funding Manager at Sennheiser, explains:

> Government grants and projects like MERCI fund high-risk, early work on DECT NR+ that individual companies cannot easily justify from product budgets. They also create a safe legal environment for collaboration, with clear frameworks for funding, intellectual property and competition law.
>
> --- Mareike Zocher, Sennheiser

OpenDECT-X provides a legally sound framework in which competitors can work on a shared foundation. Nobody carries the risk alone. The project is funded by the German Federal Ministry for Research, Technology and Space (BMFTR, grant no. 01MK26002A). That is what creates the conditions for a genuinely open, vendor-agnostic stack.

> OpenDECT-X lays the foundation for a modular and interoperable open DECT NR+ protocol stack.
>
> --- Mareike Zocher, Sennheiser

This is what technological sovereignty looks like in practice.
