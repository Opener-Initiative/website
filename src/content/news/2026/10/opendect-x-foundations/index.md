OpenDECT-X is easy to misread at a glance: a project with "DECT" in the name, three partners of very different kinds, and public funding attached to it. Put together, these three facts explain what the project actually is.

## Who is building it

OpenDECT-X brings together three partners with complementary capabilities, working toward one goal: an open ecosystem for DECT NR+ in Europe.

**Ostfalia University of Applied Sciences** coordinates the project, designs its cooperative network functions, and acts as the neutral intermediary between the consortium and external stakeholders. **Sennheiser** defines the DECT NR+ profile for professional audio applications, contributing the security and latency requirements it has built up over decades of experience in wireless audio transmission. **Last Mile Semiconductor** brings chip and hardware expertise, is responsible for the open stack architecture, and is a founding member of the DECT NR+ Interest Group.

**Nordic Semiconductor** joins as an associated partner: The first vendor to bring DECT NR+ into a commercially available System-in-Package (the nRF9161, in 2023/24), contributing SDKs and hands-on embedded experience.

This combination is no coincidence. An open standard only works if research, audio application, and chip development are involved from the outset.

## What it actually is

DECT NR+ and cordless phones share a name. That is where the similarity ends.

DECT is widely associated with the cordless phone. Its standardization began at CEPT and ETSI in 1988; the first ETSI standard, EN 300 175, was published in 1992. DECT NR+ carries a related name. It shares little else.

> DECT NR+ is a clean-sheet radio redesign using the very latest digital transmission techniques, while remaining fully co-existent with legacy DECT.
>
> --- Dr. Andreas Wilzeck, Sennheiser

In practice: a completely new radio design built on current transmission techniques, while staying compatible with the existing DECT world. Designed not for voice transmission, but for massive IoT, industrial automation, building technology, and professional audio. Since October 2021, DECT NR+ has been officially recognised by the ITU-R as a 5G technology.

Understanding this distinction matters. Those who dismiss DECT NR+ as "the cordless phone standard" are missing one of the most relevant wireless technologies for European industrial sovereignty. With OpenDECT-X, we are building the open framework that makes this restart usable for a broad ecosystem.

## Why it is publicly funded

Why does an open standard need public funding when the companies involved are financially strong? Because open-source infrastructure for industrial wireless does not emerge from a market incentive. No single company has a sufficient interest in building a reference implementation that is freely available to all, including its own competitors.

Mareike Zocher, Funding Manager at Sennheiser, explains:

> Government grants and projects like MERCI fund high-risk, early work on DECT NR+ that individual companies cannot easily justify from product budgets. They also create a safe legal environment for collaboration, with clear frameworks for funding, intellectual property and competition law.
>
> --- Mareike Zocher, Sennheiser

Public funding changes this logic. OpenDECT-X provides a legally sound framework in which competitors can work on a shared foundation. Nobody carries the risk alone. The project is funded by the German Federal Ministry for Research, Technology and Space (BMFTR, grant 01MK26002A). That is what creates the conditions for a genuinely open, vendor-agnostic stack.

> OpenDECT-X lays the foundation for a modular and interoperable open DECT NR+ protocol stack.
>
> --- Mareike Zocher, Sennheiser

This is what technological sovereignty looks like in practice.
